Quick answer

What are typical small-business valuation multiples?

BizBuySell reports that U.S. small businesses sold in 2025 averaged 2.61 times annual earnings and 0.69 times annual revenue. Industry averages ranged from 1.82 times earnings for transportation businesses to 3.25 times for online and technology businesses. These are national transaction benchmarks—not a valuation of a particular company. Normalize earnings, confirm what the sale price included, and use comparable transactions from a relevant size, market, and period.

Choose SDE, EBITDA, or revenue deliberately

  • SDE is commonly used for owner-operated businesses because it begins with earnings and adds back one owner’s compensation and supported discretionary or nonrecurring items.
  • EBITDA is more common when the buyer expects a professional management structure and owner compensation is treated as an operating cost.
  • Revenue can provide a cross-check, but it does not show margin quality. Two companies with the same sales can have very different cash flow and value.
  • Reconcile every adjustment to source records and do not mix a revenue multiple with an earnings multiple or compare unlike size bands without explanation.

Worked example: the multiple is only one input

Assume a business has $400,000 of supported normalized SDE. Applying the 2025 all-business average of 2.61× produces an illustrative indicated value of $1,044,000. That calculation does not establish market value. The result still must be reconciled to the company’s assets, debt, working capital, inventory, real estate, customer concentration, owner replacement needs, capital spending, and proposed transaction terms.

If $40,000 of a claimed add-back is actually a recurring post-closing cost, normalized SDE falls to $360,000. At the same 2.61× benchmark, the indication falls by $104,400. Evidence supporting the earnings base can matter as much as the headline multiple.

What moves a company above or below an industry average?

  • Recurring revenue, documented growth, low concentration, transferable systems, management depth, defensible margins, and clean records can support stronger positioning.
  • Owner dependence, volatile margins, customer loss, weak records, deferred capital expenditures, short leases, compliance problems, and concentrated suppliers can reduce confidence or value.
  • Company size matters because larger businesses may have deeper management, more diversified earnings, and access to different buyers and financing.
  • Cash, debt, working capital, inventory, real estate, and seller financing may be handled outside the headline multiple and must be defined in the transaction documents.

Use industry data without overstating precision

  • Record the source date, transaction type, size range, geography, earnings definition, sample size, and whether the values are asking prices or closed transactions.
  • Prefer recent closed transactions that resemble the subject company. Asking multiples show seller expectations, not completed-market evidence.
  • Use a range and explain why each comparable is relevant. A broad national sector average may not represent a specific local business.
  • Compare the market approach with an income or asset approach when the facts warrant it.

Turn a multiple range into a decision

  • Calculate low, midpoint, and high cases with the same normalized earnings base.
  • Stress-test customer loss, margin changes, owner replacement compensation, working capital, debt service, and capital needs.
  • Compare enterprise value with the seller’s equity and estimated proceeds after debt, fees, taxes, holdbacks, and transaction-specific adjustments.
  • Use the normalized EBITDA calculator to organize adjustments, the business valuation calculator to model ranges, and the seller proceeds calculator to test cash-at-closing outcomes.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good valuation multiple for a small business?

There is no universal “good” multiple. In BizBuySell’s 2025 sold-business data, the national average was 2.61× annual earnings, while sector averages ranged from 1.82× to 3.25×. A company-specific range must consider size, normalized earnings, growth, risk, transferability, assets, location, and deal terms.

What is a typical retail business valuation multiple?

BizBuySell reports 2025 averages of 2.72× earnings and 0.58× revenue for sold retail businesses. Retail subcategories vary, so verify the category, margin structure, inventory treatment, lease or real estate, and local comparable transactions before using those figures.

What is a good EBITDA multiple?

An EBITDA multiple is only meaningful relative to company size, industry, risk, growth, earnings quality, management depth, and deal terms. The BizBuySell table on this page reports SDE or cash-flow multiples, not EBITDA multiples. Do not substitute one earnings definition for the other.

Are SDE and EBITDA interchangeable?

No. They use different normalization conventions and often apply to different buyer and operator assumptions. Reconcile both calculations when the market may include owner-operators and professionally managed buyers.

Do valuation multiples include inventory?

Treatment varies. The letter of intent and purchase agreement should state whether inventory is included, separately valued, excluded, or adjusted at closing.

Can a calculator determine market value?

A calculator can organize assumptions and show sensitivity, but it does not replace market evidence, diligence, or qualified valuation advice.
