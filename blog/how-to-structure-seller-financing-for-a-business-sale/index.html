How to Structure Seller Financing for a Business Sale

Structure seller financing by defining principal, buyer cash, interest, amortization, maturity or balloon, payment frequency, security, guarantees, subordination, standby requirements, prepayment, default remedies and reporting. Model the note with senior debt and business cash flow, then have legal, tax and lending professionals document the final terms.

Start with transaction economics

  • Define which portion of consideration is paid at closing, financed by the seller, contingent, held back or adjusted after closing.
  • Test whether verified cash flow supports senior debt, seller-note payments, owner compensation, taxes and reinvestment.
  • Compare total potential proceeds with cash-at-closing; they are not the same outcome.

Negotiate the term sheet

  • Principal, rate, amortization and maturity determine payment and balloon risk.
  • Security, guarantees, covenants, reporting and default provisions determine recoverability and control.
  • Prepayment, offsets, earnouts, indemnity claims and purchase-price adjustments should not conflict with the note.

Coordinate with senior financing

  • Senior lenders may require subordination, payment restrictions or full standby for some seller debt.
  • Do not promise note payments until the lender has reviewed the structure.
  • Model intercreditor priorities and default scenarios, not only the base-case payment schedule.

Plan for tax and documentation

  • An installment sale can have specific federal tax treatment; eligibility and timing depend on the transaction and asset allocation.
  • Promissory notes, security documents, guarantees and purchase agreements must use consistent terms.
  • Both sides should obtain their own legal and tax advice before signing.

Decision checklist

VerifyTrace important assumptions to financial, legal and operating evidence.
ModelTest price, structure, financing, working capital and downside cases together.
DocumentMake terms, owners, deadlines and unresolved items explicit.

Frequently asked questions

What interest rate should a seller note use?

There is no single correct rate. The parties should consider market conditions, credit risk, security, senior-debt restrictions, tax rules and the entire purchase-price structure.

What is a balloon payment?

It is a remaining principal balance due at maturity because payments were calculated using a longer amortization period than the note term.

Should a seller note be secured?

Security is a negotiated legal issue. Available collateral, senior liens, guarantees, entity structure and enforcement costs all matter.

Can a seller note be prepaid?

The note should state whether prepayment is allowed and whether any premium or conditions apply. Coordinate the term with senior financing and tax advice.
