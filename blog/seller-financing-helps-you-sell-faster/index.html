Seller financing is one of the most practical tools available when selling a business. Instead of requiring the buyer to fund the full purchase price upfront, the seller agrees to receive part of the price over time under negotiated terms.

How Seller Financing Works in a Business Sale

A buyer might pay a portion of the purchase price at closing, then repay the remaining balance to the seller over a defined term. Terms often include interest rate, payment schedule, security rights, and default protections.

Example: a $1,000,000 deal may close with $700,000 upfront and $300,000 financed by the seller over several years.

Seller financing is often a tool to complete good deals that traditional lending cannot fully support.

Does Seller Financing Lower the Sale Price?

Not necessarily. In many deals, seller financing can support the asking price or even improve total economics because it creates flexibility buyers value.

The key is balancing upfront cash, interest income, risk, and deal quality.

Final Thoughts

Seller financing is not right for every transaction, but it can be one of the smartest tools available when used selectively and structured correctly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should a buyer put down?

It varies, but meaningful upfront equity often improves alignment and reduces seller risk.

Can seller financing help sell faster?

Yes. It can remove financing friction and widen the buyer pool.

Do sellers earn interest?

Often yes. Many seller notes include negotiated interest payments.

Is seller financing risky?

It can be if buyers are weak or documents are poorly structured.
