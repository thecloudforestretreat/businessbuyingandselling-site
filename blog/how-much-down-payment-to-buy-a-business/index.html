Quick answer

How Much Down Payment Do You Need to Buy a Business?

There is no universal down-payment percentage for every business acquisition. The amount depends on the lender, transaction size, borrower, collateral, cash flow, deal structure and current program rules. Buyers should calculate total cash needed—not just equity—including closing costs, working capital, reserves and immediate capital expenditures.

Calculate total cash needed, not a single percentage

  • Start with purchase price, then identify inventory treatment, fees, lender costs, professional fees, working capital, repairs and operating reserves.
  • Subtract committed debt, eligible seller financing and other verified sources only after their terms are known.
  • Keep a contingency for costs discovered between LOI and closing.

What changes the buyer-equity requirement?

  • Loan program and lender policy; borrower credit and liquidity; collateral; industry and operating risk; acquisition experience; customer concentration; and the strength of historical cash flow.
  • Seller notes may improve alignment, but the lender decides whether and how the note is treated.
  • Do not present an assumed equity percentage as an SBA guarantee. Confirm current requirements directly with participating lenders.

A practical sources-and-uses schedule

  • Uses: purchase consideration, inventory, transaction expenses, lender fees, working capital, repairs and reserves.
  • Sources: buyer cash, senior loan, seller note, outside equity and any permitted subordinate financing.
  • The schedule must balance, and projected cash flow must support all required payments after reasonable owner compensation and reinvestment.

Protect liquidity after closing

  • Retaining no liquidity can turn a good acquisition into an avoidable emergency.
  • Stress-test lower sales, slower collections, higher payroll, equipment failure and delayed customer transitions.
  • Keep personal emergency funds separate from documented business working capital whenever possible.

Frequently asked questions

Is 10% always enough to buy a business?

No. Some transactions may use that level, but lender policy and current program rules vary. Total cash needed can be materially higher after fees, working capital, reserves and non-financed items.

Can seller financing replace the down payment?

Sometimes it supports the capital structure, but lender treatment depends on note terms and current policy. Confirm standby, payment and subordination requirements before relying on it.

What expenses are commonly missed?

Professional fees, lender fees, inventory true-ups, deposits, insurance, licenses, payroll timing, repairs, technology transitions and working-capital needs are frequently underestimated.

Should the buyer use all available cash?

Usually a buyer should model post-close liquidity and downside scenarios before committing cash. The appropriate reserve depends on the business and financing structure.
