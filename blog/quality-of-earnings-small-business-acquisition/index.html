Quick answer

Quality of Earnings in a Small Business Acquisition

A quality-of-earnings review analyzes revenue, margins, expenses, working capital, cash conversion and proposed adjustments to assess sustainable earnings. It is not the same as a financial-statement audit and does not replace legal, tax, operational or commercial diligence. Scope should match transaction size, complexity and risk.

Reconcile the earnings base

  • Tie management statements to tax returns, general ledger, bank activity and supporting schedules for matched periods.
  • Analyze revenue recognition, cutoffs, refunds, discounts, credits and one-time transactions.
  • Bridge reported net income to EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA or SDE without losing the underlying accounts.

Test proposed add-backs

  • Determine whether each cost is nonrecurring, discretionary, owner-specific or necessary for a buyer to operate the company.
  • Estimate replacement compensation for owner responsibilities that will continue.
  • Separate documented adjustments from aspirational savings or forecast improvements.

Analyze earnings quality and cash conversion

  • Review customer, product and location concentration; churn; backlog; pricing; gross margins; payroll; vendor terms and recurring capital needs.
  • Compare earnings with operating cash flow and working-capital movements.
  • Investigate trends that depend on delayed spending, unusual collections or temporary conditions.

Translate findings into the deal

  • Update normalized earnings, valuation sensitivity, debt capacity and working-capital expectations.
  • Identify representations, covenants, holdbacks, indemnity issues or closing conditions for counsel to evaluate.
  • Track unresolved findings rather than burying them in a report appendix.

Decision checklist

VerifyTrace important assumptions to financial, legal and operating evidence.
ModelTest price, structure, financing, working capital and downside cases together.
DocumentMake terms, owners, deadlines and unresolved items explicit.

Frequently asked questions

Is a quality-of-earnings report an audit?

No. Scope and procedures differ, and a QoE review does not provide the same assurance as an audit.

Who needs a QoE review?

It can be useful when earnings adjustments, concentration, growth, working capital, accounting quality or transaction size justify deeper financial diligence.

What is an aggressive add-back?

An adjustment is aggressive when the cost is likely to recur, lacks documentation, ignores replacement cost or depends on an unproven future change.

Can a seller prepare for QoE?

Yes. Reconcile records, document adjustments, explain accounting policies and organize customer, margin, payroll and working-capital support before the process begins.
