Financial Due Diligence Checklist

Financial review determines whether the business performs as represented.

  • 3 years profit and loss statements
  • Balance sheets
  • Tax returns
  • Bank statements
  • Accounts receivable aging
  • Accounts payable aging
  • Owner add-backs with evidence
  • Cash flow consistency

Buyers should compare reported profit to real owner benefit and working capital needs.

Legal and Compliance Checklist

Corporate DocsEntity records and ownership.
LicensesCurrent and transferable.
LitigationPending disputes.
LeasesAssignment rights and terms.

Review intellectual property, permits, insurance, employment matters, and regulatory exposure where relevant.

Operational Due Diligence Checklist

Operational diligence reveals whether the business can run smoothly after ownership changes.

Owner DependenceDoes everything rely on one person? Key StaffWho is essential? SystemsModern or outdated? SuppliersSingle-source risk?

What Often Kills Deals During Diligence

Most failed deals do not collapse because of one issue. They collapse because trust erodes.

Common killers include inconsistent numbers, hidden liabilities, unrealistic seller expectations, and slow communication.

Final Thoughts

Due diligence is not paperwork for the sake of paperwork. It is the process that protects value and reveals truth before money changes hands.

The best transactions happen when both sides prepare seriously and communicate transparently.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does due diligence take?

It varies by deal size and complexity, but many transactions require several weeks.

Do sellers need due diligence too?

Yes. Sellers should prepare records and evaluate buyer credibility.

What is most important in diligence?

Reliable financials, liabilities, contracts, and operational continuity are usually core priorities.

Can diligence improve price?

Strong preparation can reduce buyer discounts and improve confidence.
