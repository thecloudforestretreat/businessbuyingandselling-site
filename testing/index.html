How Buyers Usually Value a Business

Most small businesses are valued using Seller Discretionary Earnings (SDE). Larger companies often use EBITDA. Strategic buyers may also consider synergies and growth opportunities.

However, the valuation formula is only part of the picture. The multiple applied to earnings can change significantly based on quality, systems, management depth, and industry demand.

  • SDE for owner-operated businesses
  • EBITDA for larger businesses
  • Asset value where relevant
  • Comparable recent transactions
  • Strategic acquisition premiums
  • Future cash flow potential

Why Buyers Often Discount the Price

Owners sometimes anchor to revenue or years invested. Buyers focus on future return. If uncertainty is high, they lower price or tighten terms.

The market rewards confidence and discounts risk.

Common reasons buyers discount value:

  • Messy bookkeeping
  • Declining sales trends
  • Customer concentration risk
  • Owner controls every relationship
  • Weak management bench
  • Legal or tax uncertainty

Final Thoughts

Your business is worth what the market believes it can earn for a new owner with manageable risk. That means preparation matters as much as profit.

The owners who achieve the best outcomes usually start early, improve weak spots, and run a disciplined sale process rather than guessing a number and hoping for the best.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I estimate my business value?

Start with normalized earnings, apply realistic market multiples, then adjust for risk and growth quality.

Can I increase value before selling?

Yes. Cleaner books, stronger systems, recurring revenue, and lower owner dependence often help.

Is revenue or profit more important?

Profit and transferable cash flow usually matter far more than revenue alone.

When should I prepare for sale?

Ideally six to twenty-four months before going to market.

Do all businesses sell on EBITDA?

No. Many smaller owner-operated businesses are valued using SDE instead.
